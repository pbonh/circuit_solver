## Definition

Sampling methods describe how subjects are selected from a population for study. They divide into probability sampling (random selection where each unit has a known nonzero chance) and non-probability sampling (selection based on availability or judgment).

## How It Works

- Probability methods: simple random, stratified random, systematic random, cluster sampling.
- Non-probability methods: convenience, purposive (including quota and snowball).
- Sampling errors include sampling, coverage, nonresponse, and measurement errors.

## Key Parameters

- Sample size n
- Stratification or clustering variables
- Sampling frame quality
- Response rate

## When To Use

- Probability sampling for generalizable inference
- Non-probability sampling for exploratory or hard-to-reach populations
- Stratified designs to ensure subgroup representation

## Risks & Pitfalls

- Coverage gaps systematically exclude groups
- Self-selection biases convenience samples
- Small clusters give unstable estimates
