## Definition

Oxide charge refers to the charges that reside inside or at the boundaries of a gate dielectric. Four canonical classes (per Deal terminology for Si-SiO2) are: fixed oxide charge Q_f (immobile, near the Si-SiO2 interface, typically positive), mobile-ion charge Q_m (Na+, K+ ions that drift under bias-temperature stress), oxide-trapped charge Q_ot (electrons or holes captured in oxide bulk traps, generated by radiation or stress), and interface-trapped charge Q_it (occupied interface states, energy-dependent).

## How It Works

Each charge component shifts the flatband and threshold voltages by Q / C_ox. Q_f is reduced by oxidation in dry O2 and by hydrogen anneal. Q_m is controlled by clean processing, gettering, and barrier layers. Q_ot grows under hot-carrier or radiation stress. Q_it is treated together with interface traps but is a snapshot of their occupancy at a given bias.

## Key Parameters

- Q_f, Q_m, Q_ot, Q_it (in units q/cm^2).
- Spatial distribution (sheet, distributed in oxide).
- Time-dependence (drift under stress, recovery).

## When To Use

- Diagnosing C-V hysteresis (mobile ions), bias-temperature instability (Q_ot, Q_it), radiation damage (Q_ot in MOS).

## Risks & Pitfalls

- Mobile-ion contamination at any process step can ruin a wafer.
- Hot-carrier and BTI degradation in scaled MOSFETs is fundamentally an oxide-charge phenomenon.
