## Definition

Business intelligence (BI) is the practice of turning raw operational data into actionable insights for decision-makers, typically through dashboards, reports, and visualizations. The chapter frames data visualization as the front-end through which BI delivers value.

## How It Works

A BI pipeline ingests operational and transactional data, models and aggregates it, and exposes the results through interactive dashboards and reports. Modern vendors invest equally in input data quality and output presentation, with the goal of letting users drill down quickly to identify patterns, trends, and outliers.

## Key Parameters

- Data sources and ingestion cadence
- KPIs and metrics presented on dashboards
- Refresh frequency (batch vs. real-time)
- Visualization types (charts, maps, drill-downs)

## When To Use

- Operational reporting and management dashboards
- Sales, marketing, and finance performance tracking
- Identifying improvement areas via pattern and correlation discovery
- Communicating insights consistently across teams

## Risks & Pitfalls

- Over-aggregation that hides important variation
- Vanity metrics that distract from real drivers
- Dashboards that become read-only artifacts without actionable insight
- Misleading visualizations from poor scale choices or omitted context
