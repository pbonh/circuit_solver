## Definition

Descriptive statistics summarize the main features of a dataset using numerical measures (central tendency, dispersion, shape) and visual displays. The book devotes a chapter to descriptive analysis basics before moving to inferential statistics.

## How It Works

Descriptive analysis computes measures such as mean, median, mode, variance, standard deviation, quartiles, skewness, and kurtosis. These are paired with visual tools — histograms, boxplots, scatter plots — to communicate the structure of a dataset.

## Key Parameters

- Measures of central tendency (mean, median, mode)
- Measures of dispersion (variance, SD, IQR)
- Distribution shape (skewness, kurtosis)
- Sample size

## When To Use

- First step in any data analysis to understand data shape and quality
- Reporting baseline characteristics of populations

## Risks & Pitfalls

- A single summary can hide bimodality or outliers
- Comparing means without checking dispersion
- Using mean on heavily skewed data
