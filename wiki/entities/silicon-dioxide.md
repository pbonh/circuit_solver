## Overview

Silicon dioxide is the amorphous gate dielectric that enabled the planar MOS technology of the modern microelectronics industry. Native to silicon (any clean Si surface oxidizes), thermally grown SiO2 is chemically inert, mechanically robust, and produces an exceptionally low interface-trap density (~1e10 cm^-2 eV^-1 after hydrogen anneal) with the underlying Si.

## Characteristics

- Bandgap ~9 eV; barrier to Si conduction band ~3.1 eV (electrons), ~4.7 eV (holes).
- Dielectric constant 3.9 (relative).
- Density ~2.2 g/cm^3 (thermally grown).
- Breakdown field ~10 MV/cm in pristine thin films; reduced by defects.
- Amorphous structure (no grain boundaries).

## Common Strategies

- Dry oxidation (lower D_it) vs. wet oxidation (faster growth) of silicon at 800-1100 deg C.
- Post-oxidation hydrogen anneal to passivate dangling bonds.
- Nitridation to form SiON gate dielectrics that resist boron penetration and improve TDDB.
- Largely replaced by HfO2 high-k stacks below 1 nm equivalent oxide thickness in modern CMOS, though SiO2 remains the reference dielectric.
