## Overview

Silicon is the dominant semiconductor material of the electronics industry. It is a group-IV element with a diamond crystal structure and an indirect bandgap of 1.12 eV at 300 K. Virtually all integrated circuits, the majority of solar cells, many power devices, and most MEMS structures are built on silicon substrates.

## Characteristics

- Crystal structure: diamond cubic; lattice constant a = 5.43 A.
- Bandgap: 1.12 eV at 300 K (indirect, conduction-band minimum along [100] in six equivalent valleys).
- Electron / hole mobility (pure, 300 K): ~1450 / 450 cm^2/V-s.
- Saturation velocity: ~1e7 cm/s.
- Intrinsic carrier density: ~1e10 cm^-3 at 300 K.
- Thermal conductivity: ~1.5 W/cm-K.
- LO phonon energy: 63 meV.
- Optical-phonon-limited mobility T-dependence: ~T^-2.42 (electrons), T^-2.20 (holes).

## Common Strategies

- Doping with P, As, Sb (donors) or B, Al, Ga (acceptors); deep impurities (Au, Pt) used for lifetime control.
- High-purity (float-zone or Czochralski) wafers for ICs.
- Thermal oxidation produces high-quality SiO2 — the property that enabled MOS technology.
- Strained-Si layers on SiGe for enhanced mobility.
