## Overview

The semiconductor device family is the taxonomy of devices built around junctions in semiconductors. Sze and Ng organize it into five groups: building blocks (p-n junction, metal-semiconductor contact, MIS capacitor), transistors (BJT, MOSFET, JFET/MESFET/MODFET), negative-resistance and power devices (tunnel/resonant-tunneling diodes, IMPATT/BARITT/TUNNETT, transferred-electron and real-space-transfer devices, thyristor/IGBT), and photonic devices (LEDs, lasers, photodetectors, solar cells) plus sensors for non-electrical signals.

## Characteristics

- Common substrate: doped semiconductor crystal (Si dominant, GaAs and other III-V for photonic / high-speed).
- Operation by combinations of drift, diffusion, recombination, generation, thermionic emission, tunneling, impact ionization.
- Performance described by current-voltage characteristics, frequency response, noise, breakdown, and reliability metrics.

## Common Strategies

- Devices are often hybrids of two simpler structures (MOS-controlled thyristor = MOSFET + thyristor; IGBT = MOSFET + BJT).
- Heterojunctions add band-engineering freedom (HBT, HEMT, quantum-well lasers).
- Power devices use lightly doped drift regions; high-speed devices use thin bases, short channels, and high-mobility materials.
