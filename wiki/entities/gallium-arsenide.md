## Overview

Gallium arsenide is a III-V compound semiconductor with a zincblende crystal structure and a direct bandgap of 1.42 eV at 300 K. Its direct gap makes it the workhorse material for visible-near-IR lasers and LEDs, and its high electron mobility plus the transferred-electron effect make it valuable for microwave and high-speed devices.

## Characteristics

- Crystal structure: zincblende; lattice constant a = 5.65 A.
- Bandgap: 1.42 eV at 300 K (direct, minimum at Gamma).
- Electron / hole mobility (pure, 300 K): ~8500 / 400 cm^2/V-s.
- Saturation velocity: ~6e6 cm/s, but with negative differential mobility above ~3 kV/cm (transferred-electron effect).
- LO phonon energy: 35 meV.
- Higher-energy [111] satellite valley ~0.3 eV above the Gamma minimum; m_e* ~ 0.063 m_0 (Gamma) vs ~0.55 m_0 (L valley).
- Cleavage on {110} planes.
- Thermal conductivity significantly lower than Si.

## Common Strategies

- AlGaAs / GaAs and InGaAs / GaAs heterojunctions for HEMT, HBT, lasers, LEDs.
- Modulation doping to achieve high-mobility 2DEG in HEMTs.
- Direct-bandgap optoelectronic emitters (lasers and LEDs) at 850 nm and via alloying at other wavelengths.
- Used as a substrate for further III-V epitaxy.
