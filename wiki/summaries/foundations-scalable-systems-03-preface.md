## Key Points
- Thesis: the ability of software systems to operate at scale is an increasingly defining factor of success, especially for internet-facing systems.
- Two essential complications drive the entire book: (1) partial failures of components in an otherwise correct system, and (2) the coordination of many moving parts so that one slow or broken component doesn't sink the whole system.
- Target audience is software engineers and architects with limited prior exposure to distributed/concurrent systems who need both theoretical and practical design knowledge.
- The book promises coverage of distributed-system fundamentals (state, time, communication, coordination, concurrency), service-tier scalability tactics, distributed database operation, and event/stream platforms such as Apache Kafka and Apache Flink.
- Content was developed alongside the author's advanced undergraduate / graduate course at Northeastern University (CS6650 Building Scalable Distributed Systems).
- Acknowledges intellectual debts to Jon Kerridge, Matt Bass, John Klein, and CMU SEI; reviewers include Mark Richards and Matt Stine.
- Code examples live at https://oreil.ly/fss-git-repo; the book's web page is https://oreil.ly/scal-sys.
