## Key Points

- Part I (Chapter 1) summarizes the semiconductor physics (energy bands, carrier concentration, transport) used throughout the book, with emphasis on Si and GaAs.
- Part II (Chapters 2-4) covers the basic device building blocks: p-n junction (and heterojunction), metal-semiconductor contact (Schottky and ohmic), and the MIS/MOS capacitor — the building blocks from which all semiconductor devices are constructed.
- Part III (Chapters 5-7) covers the transistor family: bipolar transistors (potential-effect), MOSFETs (dominant device for advanced ICs, microprocessors, DRAM, and nonvolatile memory), and the JFET/MESFET/MODFET family used in high-speed and power applications.
- Part IV (Chapters 8-11) covers negative-resistance and power devices: tunnel diodes (including resonant-tunneling), transit-time devices (IMPATT, BARITT, TUNNETT), transferred-electron devices (TED) and real-space-transfer devices, and thyristors / MOS-controlled thyristors / IGBTs.
- Part V (Chapters 12-14) covers photonic devices and sensors: LEDs and lasers, photodetectors and solar cells, and sensors for thermal/mechanical/magnetic/chemical signals.
- The p-n junction is identified as the universal building block of semiconductor devices, making it the foundational topic for everything that follows.
- The MOSFET is identified as the most-important device for advanced integrated circuits.
- A sensor is defined as a device that detects or measures an external signal; six fundamental signal classes are electrical, optical, thermal, mechanical, magnetic, and chemical.
- Each chapter in Parts III-V is largely self-contained after Parts I and II are read.
