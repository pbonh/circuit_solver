## Key Points

- The chapter covers two-terminal and three-terminal devices whose operation depends on quantum-mechanical tunneling: the Esaki tunnel diode, the backward diode, MIS tunnel devices, MIM (metal-insulator-metal) tunnel diodes, hot-electron transistors, and the resonant-tunneling diode (RTD).
- Tunnel diode: a heavily doped (degenerate) p-n junction with a very narrow depletion region (<10 nm). Forward bias from 0 to V_p produces tunneling of conduction-band electrons on the n-side into empty valence-band states on the p-side; beyond V_p the available states diminish and the current falls (negative differential resistance) until normal diffusion current takes over at V_v.
- Tunneling probability follows WKB across the narrow junction; current = integral over energy of (transmission) x (n-side supply) x (1 - p-side occupancy); peak-to-valley current ratio (PVCR) is the figure of merit (2.4 in GaAs, 8 in Ge, > 60 in SiGe interband, ~30 in InP RTD).
- Tunnel diodes were the first solid-state devices to demonstrate microwave amplification and oscillation (Esaki 1958). Used in low-noise microwave amplifiers and oscillators historically; today dominant applications are as voltage references at low current and as functional devices (logic with reduced transistor count).
- Backward diode: a slightly less heavily doped diode operated with reverse bias as the "on" state and forward as the "off" state -- the opposite of an ordinary diode. Used as a square-law microwave detector and mixer because of its strong I-V curvature near zero bias.
- MIS tunnel devices: thin-oxide MIS structures pass tunneling current with selectivity between majority and minority carriers; used in solar-cell and switching applications.
- MIS switch diode: bistable I-V from interface-state charging.
- MIM tunnel diodes: very thin (1-3 nm) oxide between two metals; used in detectors, terahertz rectennas, and antenna-coupled tunnel diodes for far-IR detection.
- Hot-electron transistors: vertical three-terminal devices in which carriers cross a narrow base ballistically; bipolar inversion transistor (BICFET) and tunnel-emitter HBTs explored as ultrafast structures but never widely deployed.
- Resonant-tunneling diode (RTD): a double-barrier heterostructure encloses a quantum well; transmission peaks at the well's bound-state energy, producing sharp negative-differential-resistance regions. Used in millimeter-wave oscillators (above 1 THz with InAs/AlSb), multi-valued logic, and analog-to-digital converters.
