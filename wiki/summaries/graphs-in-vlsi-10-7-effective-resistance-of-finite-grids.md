## Key Points

- Chapter 6's half-/quarter-plane image method handles a single boundary or corner; this chapter extends it via the Infinity Mirror Technique to finite rectangular grids where both x and y dimensions are bounded.
- Conceptually: reflecting an infinite strip across its left boundary doubles its width while preserving the field within the original strip; iterating this reflection in both directions creates a pattern analogous to placing an object between two parallel mirrors (hence "infinity mirror"). The image current sources are now indexed by integer pairs (i, j) covering an infinite lattice of mirror copies.
- Closed-form effective-resistance expressions are derived in three increasingly constrained settings: infinite strip (finite x, infinite y), semi-infinite strip (finite x, half-infinite y), and finite mesh (both bounded).
- For a finite mesh, the effective resistance R_{w_x, w_y} between (x_0, y_0) and (x, y) is a double infinite sum over (i, j) of 10 Φ_k terms evaluated at translated coordinates. In practice 4 terms in each direction (N = M = 4) give 0.3% accuracy.
- Generalization to n dimensions: source and target each have 2^n primary images (sign-flip patterns across each axis), and the full image set is generated by translating each image by 2 w ◦ a for all integer vectors a.
- Closed-form approximation of Φ_k uses the polynomial-in-k form from Chapter 6 but falls back to a lookup table for small (x, y) where the approximation error spikes; the hybrid achieves ~1% worst-case accuracy.
- Case study 1 (mesh reduction): A large grid is reduced to a small one over only the nodes of interest using G^† = (1/2)(−R_S − (1/n) 1_{n,n} R_S + R_S 1_{n,n} + (2/n²) 1_{n,1} R_S 1_{1,n}). 17× speedup for 0.23% nodes of interest; 1400× speedup with 100 nodes of interest in a 10^3 × 10^4 grid.
- Case study 2 (capacitive touch screen): The cathode electrode is modeled as a finite mesh; effective-resistance estimates match Q3D extractions while dramatically reducing computation.
- Case study 3 (substrate noise in mixed-signal CMOS): A 3D resistive mesh model captures coupling from digital ground to analog ground through the substrate. The Infinity Mirror Technique enables fast evaluation of how substrate-contact spacing affects analog-ground noise V_ga = I R_ga R_gd / (R_gd + R_s + R_ga); ~10× speedup over nodal analysis on a 200×200×10 μm grid.
