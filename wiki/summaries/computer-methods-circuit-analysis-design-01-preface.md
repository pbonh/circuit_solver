## Key Points

- Covers appropriate portions of circuit theory plus the mathematical and computational techniques required for computer-aided analysis and design of electronic circuits.
- 17 chapters total: Chapters 1–10 focus on linear-circuit theory and computational methods; Chapters 11–13 deal with nonlinear analysis; Chapter 14 extends linear methods to digital and switched-capacitor networks; Chapters 15–17 address design of linear/nonlinear circuits, including optimization (Ch. 15), steady-state of periodically excited networks (Ch. 16), and CAD examples (Ch. 17).
- About 60% of the book is suitable for a second/third undergraduate circuit-theory course; the entire book is suitable for graduate courses or self-study. Assumes sophomore-level calculus, algebra, programming, and circuit theory.
- Two bibliographies: numbered chapter-end references for in-text citations, plus a general supplementary list at the end of the book. Software routines are included in Appendices D and E.
- Appendix D is a FORTRAN program for linear network analysis implementing most of the material of Chapters 1–10; Appendix E gives a sparse matrix package.
- Larger realistic examples were solved by WATAND (Waterloo Analysis and Design), an interactive package with advanced graphics, developed at the University of Waterloo.
- Acknowledges contributions from Profs. H. K. Kesavan, S. G. Chamberlain, P. R. Bryant (Waterloo), N. K. Bose (Pittsburgh), S. K. Mitra (UC), Dr. A. E. Ruehli (IBM), and Martin Vlach.
