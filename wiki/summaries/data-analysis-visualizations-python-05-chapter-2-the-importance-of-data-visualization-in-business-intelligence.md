## Key Points

- Defines data visualization as the pictorial/graphical presentation of data and frames it as essential to extracting decision-making value from big data.
- Argues that BI vendors now focus on both ingestion quality and output presentation, with dashboards as the canonical compiler of multiple visualizations.
- Lists business goals of visualization: interactive graphs, dashboards, faster decisions, pattern/trend/correlation discovery, organizing massive data, and richer presentation of findings.
- Projects the future of visualization as a science discipline supporting network theory, IoT, complexity theory, nanoscience, and social-network analysis.
- Lists five concrete business benefits: faster response times, simplicity, easier pattern visualization, team involvement, and unified interpretation.
- Compares R and Python head-to-head: R favored by scholars/researchers with statistical libraries (Tidyverse, ggplot2, Caret, Zoo); Python favored for deployment, production, and integration (Pandas, NumPy, SciPy, scikit-learn, TensorFlow).
- Distinguishes exploration (extract information) from explanation (communicate information) as the two basic types of visualization and lists 2D categories (temporal, multidimensional, hierarchical, network).
- Walks through package management with `pip` and `conda` (search, install, upgrade, list) including in-notebook install-or-import patterns.
- Surveys Python visualization libraries: Pygal, Altair, VisPy, PyQtGraph, Matplotlib, Bokeh, Seaborn, Plotly, ggplot, and Geoplotlib.
- Demonstrates Matplotlib basics: `%matplotlib inline` vs `notebook`, scatter plots with custom markers/colors, axis ranges, titles/labels, and overlaid sine/cosine line plots.
- Demonstrates Seaborn statistical plots: `distplot`, `kdeplot` (1D and 2D), `jointplot` (kernel and hex), and `pairplot` for multidimensional exploration.
- Shows Plotly for online/offline interactive graphs (2D histogram contour heatmap, scatter, init_notebook_mode usage).
- Demonstrates Pandas DataFrame `.plot` shortcuts: line, bar (vertical/horizontal/stacked), histogram (with bin control), per-column `.hist`, box, area, scatter, and pie via `subplots=True`.
- Closes with an exercise constructing a sales DataFrame and producing bar, pie, box, area, and stacked-bar visualizations.
